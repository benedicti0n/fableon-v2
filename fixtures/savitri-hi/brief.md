## Brief: Savitri and Satyavan — the princess who out-argued Death

**Audience:** Curious adults scrolling a vertical feed. Assume they know nothing about the Mahabharata episode and will bail in three seconds if it feels like homework. They like myth, true-crime pacing, and cleverness rewarded.

**Tone:** Deadpan and witty, in the register of the reference film — flat, confident delivery; jokes carried by timing and understatement, never by winking at the camera. The narrator treats cosmic stakes as a mildly inconvenient admin problem. Warmth sneaks in at the end; no sentimentality before that.

**Hook options (pick one):**
1. "A princess married a man with exactly one year to live. She had read the fine print."
2. "Death came to collect. He left with a signed agreement and no soul."
3. "This is the story of the only person who ever beat Death in a conversation — and she did it by asking questions."

**Must-include points:**
- Savitri's choice: she picks Satyavan knowingly, against the astrologer's warning — agency, not fate.
- The year of quiet preparation; she is not passive, she is studying.
- Satyavan's death in the forest and the arrival of Yama, god of death.
- The core engine: she doesn't fight, she *negotiates* — a series of boons, each one carefully worded, each one Yama granting before he sees the trap closing.
- The final ask that wins Satyavan back, and why Yama concedes.
- One clean closing line that reframes the myth: intelligence as the weapon, patience as the strategy.

**Things to avoid:**
- Devotional or sermonizing framing; no "ancient wisdom teaches us."
- A long cast list or family-tree exposition — keep to Savitri, Satyavan, Yama.
- Generic "girl boss" gloss; her win is rhetorical precision, not girl-power sloganeering.
- Visual clutter: no crowded deity tableaux that read as mush on a phone screen.
- Spoiling the boon trap in the hook; let the structure do the work.
- Modern-slang jokes that will date the piece.

**Target length:** 90 seconds (~210–240 spoken words at a deadpan pace, leaving room for two or three beats of silence on punchlines).
