# "Book One" — an Adi Parva short, original script v1 (2026-11-15)
Format: ~120 s vertical short (user asked for 120 s), one steady narrator, our own characters and wording.
Topic: the Adi Parva ("The Book of the Beginning"), the first of the 18 books of the Mahabharata.

## Facts (fact-checked by an Opus verifier against Ganguli excerpts, 2026-11-15; source: The Mahabharata, Adi Parva, K. M. Ganguli translation 1883-1896, public domain; section numbers approx.)
1. Vyasa asks Ganesha to write the poem down. Ganesha agrees only if his pen never stops; Vyasa counters that Ganesha
   must not write anything he does not understand. Vyasa then knits "knots" of very close composition (the text says
   8,800 such verses), and while Ganesha pauses to think, Vyasa composes more. (Adi Parva, Sec. I)
2. King Shantanu falls for Satyavati, daughter of the chief of the fishermen. Her father demands that her son be king.
   Prince Devavrata (Shantanu's son by Ganga) renounces the throne; when the father asks about Devavrata's future sons,
   he vows lifelong celibacy. Flowers rain from the sky and he is called Bhishma ("the terrible", for the terrible vow).
   Shantanu grants him the boon that death will not come to him unless he wishes it. (Sec. C)
3. Satyavati's earlier son is Vyasa. Vyasa fathers Dhritarashtra (born blind), Pandu and Vidura on the widows of
   Vichitravirya, so he is the grandfather of both the Kauravas and the Pandavas. (Sec. CV-CVI)
4. Pandu shoots a deer coupling with its mate; it is the sage Kindama, who curses him to die if he unites with his wife.
   (Sec. CXVIII)
5. Kunti has a mantra (from the sage Durvasa) to summon any god for a son: Yudhishthira (Dharma), Bhima (Vayu, the Wind),
   Arjuna (Indra). She lets Madri, Pandu's second wife, use it once: Madri calls the twin Ashvins and has the twins
   Nakula and Sahadeva. (Sec. CXI, CXXII-CXXIV)
6. Gandhari (who blindfolds herself for life to share her husband's blindness) carries her pregnancy for two years and
   delivers a hard mass of flesh. Vyasa has it divided into 101 pieces, each put into a pot of clarified butter (ghee);
   from them come 100 sons (Duryodhana first) and one daughter, Duhshala. (Sec. CX, CXV)
7. As boys, Bhima bullies the Kauravas: drags them by the hair, holds them under water, and when they climb trees for
   fruit he kicks the tree so they fall. Duryodhana poisons him, binds him and throws him into the Ganga; Bhima sinks to
   the Naga (snake) realm, the snakes' venom cancels the poison, and he drinks eight jars of their nectar and returns
   stronger. (Sec. CXXVIII)
8. Duryodhana has a house built at Varanavata of lac and other combustibles to burn the Pandavas. Warned by Vidura, they
   dig a tunnel, Bhima sets the house on fire, they escape, and everyone believes they died. (Sec. CXLIII-CXLVIII)
9. Disguised as Brahmanas, the Pandavas attend Draupadi's swayamvara: string a very stiff bow and hit a mark set high on a
   machine with five arrows. The kings fail and are thrown down by the bow. Arjuna strings it and hits the mark.
   (Sec. CLXXXVI-CLXXXIX)
10. The brothers announce Draupadi to Kunti as the alms they received; from inside, without looking, Kunti says
   "Enjoy ye all (what ye have obtained)". Her word must not be untrue, and Draupadi marries all five. (Sec. CXCI-CXCVIII)
11. The kingdom is divided; the Pandavas get Khandavaprastha, a wilderness, and build Indraprastha. (Sec. CCVII)
12. Agni, the fire god, has drunk ghee for twelve years at King Swetaki's sacrifice and lost his appetite and lustre;
   Brahma tells him the cure is to consume the Khandava forest. Krishna and Arjuna help him, and Agni gives Arjuna the
   bow Gandiva. (Sec. CCXXIII-CCXXVI)

## Script (voice, ~450 words; fact-check v1 fixes applied)
Legend says the longest poem ever written had a god for a typist.
Vyasa dictated, Ganesha wrote, on one condition: Vyasa could never stop talking.
His counter: Ganesha had to understand every verse first.
So whenever Vyasa needed time, he'd drop a verse so knotty even a god had to stop and think.
Our story starts with King Shantanu falling for a fisherman's daughter.
Her father's one demand: her son becomes king.
Problem: the king already has the perfect heir, Prince Devavrata.
Devavrata gives up the throne. Her father: sure, but what about your kids?
So he vows lifelong celibacy.
The gods rain down flowers and rename him Bhishma: the one with the terrible vow.
Luckily, his father grants him a gift: he can choose the day he dies.
Unluckily, he lives long enough to see what happens next.
A generation later: Dhritarashtra, born blind, and his brother Pandu, the king.
Pandu shoots a deer that's secretly a sage. The curse: lie with your wife, and die.
Conveniently, his wife Kunti knows a chant that summons gods for sons.
She calls three gods, then lends the chant to Pandu's second wife: twins.
Five brothers: the Pandavas.
Meanwhile, the blind prince's wife is pregnant for two years, then delivers a lump of flesh.
Vyasa divides it into a hundred and one pieces, each in its own jar of ghee.
Out come a hundred brothers, the Kauravas, and one sister.
Five cousins, a hundred cousins, one palace.
Bhima, the strongest Pandava, keeps shaking the Kauravas out of fruit trees.
So Duryodhana, the eldest Kaurava, poisons him and throws him in the river.
Bhima sinks to the snake kingdom, drinks eight jars of nectar, and comes back stronger.
Next, Duryodhana builds them a holiday home out of lacquer, so it'll burn.
They get a tunnel dug, torch it themselves, and sneak off while everyone thinks they're dead.
In disguise, they enter Princess Draupadi's wedding contest: string a monster bow, hit a target in the sky.
Kings fail. Some get thrown flat by the bow.
Arjuna strings it in a blink and hits the mark.
They run home: Mother, look what alms we got!
Kunti, without looking: share it between you.
Her word had to stand, so Draupadi married all five.
You see, here a promise works like a sent message. There's no unsend button.
Bhishma's vow, Kunti's share it, even Ganesha's deal: every word gets kept, whatever it costs.
The kingdom splits. The Pandavas get a wilderness and build a city there anyway.
The forest next door? The fire god burns it down to cure his indigestion. Too much ghee.
Oh, and Vyasa, the sage dictating all of this? He's grandfather to both sides.
