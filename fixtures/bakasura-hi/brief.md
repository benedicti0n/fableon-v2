## Story Brief — *Bhima and Bakasura* (vertical short, ~90s)

**Audience:** Curious adults scrolling a vertical feed. They know nothing about the Mahabharata and don't need to — they need a reason to stop scrolling in the first second and a reason to stay for the punchline. Assume intelligence, zero prior context.

**Tone:** Deadpan, witty, dry-as-a-bone narration in the register of the reference film. The humor comes from treating an epic mythological event like a mildly inconvenient logistical problem. Understatement over exclamation. No winking at the camera, no "epic music" cues, no narrator who's impressed with himself. The absurdity is delivered flat, as fact.

**Hook options (pick one):**
1. *"The village had a demon problem. The solution was to send one man to be eaten. He had other plans for the menu."*
2. *"Once a month, a town fed a demon a human being. This month, they sent the wrong human being."*
3. *"Bakasura had a very simple arrangement: one meal, once a month. Then Bhima showed up, and the arrangement got complicated."*

**Must-include points:**
- The arrangement: a town, a demon (Bakasura), a monthly tribute of one person plus a cartload of food — the deal, stated plainly as a bureaucratic fact.
- Who Bhima is, in one line: the strong one, currently incognito, currently hungry.
- The swap: Bhima volunteers/goes in place of the tribute — frame it as him seeing a free meal, not heroism.
- The twist the title promises: he eats the demon's dinner first. The demon arrives to find his food gone. This is the comedic center — give it room.
- The fight: brief, lopsided, physically specific (he's a strongman; the demon is used to victims who cooperate).
- The aftermath: the town's reaction, deadpan. The arrangement ends. Nobody sends anyone to be eaten again.

**Things to avoid:**
- No devotional or awe-struck framing; no swelling-score reverence.
- No over-explaining the Mahabharata or the Pandavas' exile — one clause of context, maximum.
- No gore, no lingering on violence; keep it cartoonish and bloodless.
- No modern-slang jokes or meme captions — the wit should come from the writing, not from borrowed internet voice.
- No narrator moral at the end. Land the joke and cut.
- Don't spoil the dinner twist in the hook if you use option 1 or 3 — let it arrive.

**Target length:** ~50 seconds (roughly 165–185 words of narration, allowing for short beats of silence and visual gags). Keep it tight; vertical feeds punish slack.

**Hook shape:** sentence 1 must state the central paradox as a flat fact: the demon demanded a dinner, and the dinner ate him first. Do not open with the town, the tribute system, the Pandavas or any backstory. The payoff must call back to that exact idea.
