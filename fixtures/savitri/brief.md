## Brief: *Savitri and Satyavan* — the princess who out-argued Death

**Audience:** Curious adults scrolling a vertical feed. Assume intelligence, low patience, zero prior knowledge of the Mahabharata. They should leave feeling they learned something real and were entertained getting there.

**Tone:** Deadpan and witty, in the register of the reference film — dry narration, no winking at the camera, jokes delivered flat and fast. Comedy comes from understatement and the absurd logic of the situation (a woman negotiating with the god of death like it's a customer service call), not from mugging, sound-effect gags, or meme slang. Respect the myth; let the wit come from framing, not mockery.

**Hook options (pick one):**
1. *"A princess married a man prophesied to die in exactly one year. Her plan was not to grieve. It was to read the fine print."*
2. *"The god of death came to collect a soul and left without it — because he made the mistake of granting her a wish."*
3. *"Everyone remembers she saved her husband. Nobody remembers she did it by being extremely, patiently annoying."*

**Must-include:**
- Who Savitri and Satyavan are, in two or three lines max (princess who chose her own husband; he's doomed to die in a year; she knows).
- The setup: she marries him anyway, and the year passes.
- The core scene: Yama arrives, takes Satyavan's soul, she follows. This is the spine of the piece — give it the most room.
- The mechanism of the win: she earns boons through her answers, and the final boon is worded so cleverly it can't be refused. Make the loophole legible — the audience must *get* the trick, not just be told it happened.
- The payoff: Death concedes, husband lives, and the closing beat lands the idea that she won with words, not weapons.

**Avoid:**
- A full cast of characters or genealogy — no uncle-of-a-cousin exposition.
- Sermonizing about duty, fate, or devotion; let the story carry it.
- Generic "girl power" voiceover or moralizing tag line.
- Anachronistic meme slang, emoji energy, or punchline-stacked narration.
- Spoiling the loophole in the hook — the reveal is the reason to keep watching.

**Target length:** ~90 seconds. Roughly 210–240 words of narration at a brisk vertical pace, or 8–10 short beats. Front-load the hook in the first 3 seconds; land the twist by ~70 seconds; leave ~15 seconds for the closing beat.
